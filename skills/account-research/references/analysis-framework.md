# Analysis framework — have an opinion

The user asked for analysis, not stenography. When you surface anything, interpret it. This file is
how. The failure mode to avoid: pasting a list of headlines or opportunities with no point of view.

## The "so what?" rule
Every fact you report gets a consequence. Pattern: **Observation → Implication → Recommendation.**
> "Their CFO left last month (obs). New CFOs often freeze discretionary spend and re-bid vendors
> (impl). Our renewal is in Q3 — get ahead of it; line up an exec sponsor now (rec)."

If you can't articulate a "so what," it's probably ⚪ noise — say so and move on.

## Signal vs. noise triage
Route every development into one of three buckets (this maps to `signals.md`):

- **🔴 Pay attention** — materially affects the relationship, a live deal, renewal, or our
  positioning. Leadership change in our buying center, budget/reorg news, a competitor displacement,
  a churn risk, an expansion trigger. → State the recommended action.
- **🟡 Watch** — plausibly relevant, not yet actionable. Strategy shifts, hiring trends, sector
  moves. → Note what would promote it to 🔴.
- **⚪ Noise** — real but immaterial to us: generic PR, distant product news, stock chatter with no
  operational read-through. → Log one line + why dismissed, so it isn't re-litigated.

Be willing to say "this week is mostly noise." That's a valid, useful answer.

### Noise tells
Award-listicles, undated content marketing, stock-price micro-moves, news about a parent/unrelated
namesake, anything you can't tie to a person, budget, deal, or decision in this account.

## SWOT — make it about *us and this account*, not generic
Not "the company is big (strength)." Tie each item to the relationship and, where possible, a source.

- **Strengths** — our position/relationship advantages, or their health that helps our case.
  *e.g. "3 active champions across IT + Ops [Slack #coupe-deal]; strong renewal history."*
- **Weaknesses** — gaps in our footprint or their constraints working against us.
  *e.g. "Single-threaded on one VP; no economic-buyer relationship."*
- **Opportunities** — concrete openings to pursue. *e.g. "They just stood up a data team → Data Cloud angle."*
- **Threats** — risks to the account. *e.g. "Competitor named in their RFP [Drive: RFP.pdf]; budget scrutiny post-reorg."*

End every SWOT with:
- **BLUF** at the top (2–4 sentences: where we stand + the #1 next move).
- **Where I'd place my bets** — prioritized, opinionated.
- **30 / 60 / 90-day plays** — specific and assignable.

## Brainstorming
Use loaded memory as fuel (profile priorities, open opps, signals). Generate options, then *rank*
them by fit and effort — don't just spray ideas. Capture the good ones in a `research/` note with a
recommendation, and log the session. Push back if an idea conflicts with what memory already knows.

## Calibration & honesty
- Distinguish **fact** (sourced) from **inference** (your read) — label the latter.
- Flag staleness: "pipeline last synced 3 weeks ago — re-sync before trusting."
- Surface **contradictions** across sources; a mismatch (web vs. SFDC vs. Slack) is itself signal.
- Don't manufacture urgency. If nothing changed, say so.
